## 2. Artist bio — LONG (third person, ~210 words)

Charlie "Blackcat" Smith (b. 1966, Columbus, Ohio) is an Atlanta-based sculptor, metal fabricator, and licensed flame-effects artist who has spent nearly three decades building large-scale interactive fire art. A graduate of the Atlanta College of Art (BFA, 1992) and the founder of Sparseland Studios, he is known at Burning Man as "Uncle Charlie" — and to many as "the Johnny Appleseed of Burning Man" for the way he travels, teaches communities to weld and fabricate, and builds fire sculptures *with* people rather than for them.

His work is unmistakable: mythic animal and deity effigies in raw and stainless steel — jackal-headed roosters, a metal Ganesha, a rotund clown, a seven-pointed star — that pair wood-fired cauldrons with LPG flame effects and are powered by the physical energy of the crowd. Major pieces include the *Synapses* six-city cauldron tour (2005), *Contraptions* (2007), the rideable *Fleeble Flobbler* (2008), the *24/7 A Time Star* / "Timing Is Everything" (Burning Man 2012; later a beloved Atlanta BeltLine landmark), and *Uncle Charlie's Red Hot Cock* (2018). He helped instigate AfrikaBurn and co-produced Miami's Love Burn. His 2026 project, *The Burning Book of Brian*, is a community-built memorial to writer Brian Doherty. Through it all, his subject is the same: community, transformation, and fire as the thing that gathers us.

## 3. The Burning Book of Brian — fundraising / press blurb (warm; semi-voice)

**The Burning Book of Brian — a memorial fire at Burning Man 2026.**

When Brian Doherty died this spring, the Burning Man community lost the man who literally wrote the book on it — *This Is Burning Man*. Charlie "Uncle Charlie" Smith met Brian at HEARTH back in 2000, and he's building him a goodbye worthy of the friendship: a 15-foot steel-and-fire effigy of an open book, a human heart pulsing flame at its core — a literal heartbeat for the playa to gather around.

It's being built the way Charlie builds everything — together. Eight to ten artists are skinning its pages with words and symbols for Brian; the rest of us bring the wood, the hands, and the heart. On Wednesday night we'll process out from the Cacophony Society Cocktail Hour and burn it once, solemn and joyful both — Brian would've wanted it both ways.

Help us feed the fire. Every dollar, log, and pair of hands goes into the burn. **Donate: PayPal.Me/thewakingbird** · build with us at Sparseland Studio, Atlanta.

## 4. Grant boilerplate (third person, fundable; ~130 words)

Sparseland Studios, led by artist Charlie "Blackcat" Smith, creates large-scale interactive fire sculpture through community-built workshops. For 25 years, our practice has used metal fabrication and fire as tools for connection: participants of all skill levels learn to weld, design, and operate monumental artworks together, then carry both the skills and the sense of belonging back into their communities — a model proven across six U.S. cities (the 2005 Synapses tour), at the first AfrikaBurn, and through repeated Burning Man honoraria since 2000. Our work prioritizes radical inclusion, gifting, mentorship of emerging artists, and rigorous public safety (licensed flame-effects operation and full Leave-No-Trace practice). We respectfully request support to continue building participatory public art that turns spectators into makers and strangers into community.
