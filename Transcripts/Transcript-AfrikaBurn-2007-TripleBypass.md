**[The name]**
Welcome to the Quad Pass project — which now has been renamed "Triple Bypass." It's nearly given us all a heart attack. Oh my God, I can't even believe it's really happening. Amazingly, right now we're at the point in the Quad Pass project building stage where we're a little over halfway, and we're in the zone where we're going to be doing continuous welds. That's where we're at. We're doing great. I want to have one team today building boxes.

**[The tour / the method]**
Jamie Ladet and myself have been touring around the United States of America building fire cauldrons with the communities — based on gift economy. As part of our gift, we've been teaching people how to realize a sculptural form, in order to get them to do what they want to do with their art forms and their ideas, and together to see how community can link.

The symbolism is you. We're asking you — we want to see what you guys are thinking. It's on you all. We're here to help do that. It's a room, it's a totem, it's a piece [of] history. Thematically here with Africa Burns there are no spectators, and so what we have is full-on collaboration — everybody getting involved on this together. I love people; I want to share. That's why we're here, that's why we work so hard to be here. We've been working for over a year and a half to get here.

**[Build craft]**
First we tack it all on, hold it in place, then we come back, weld it all, and then cut it all off. We're not really familiar with the metric system, so we made a little miscalculation — it's about twice as big as we would normally order.

**[Fire as material]**
Africa Burns has been quite a new development — taking the artistic form of the fire, and the fire cauldron, and the fire energy into another level, from the Nevada desert to the deserts of South Africa. For about over 12 years now I've been playing with fire inside of the metal structure — the element of fire breathing through the passageways. The passageways of the fire are the Earth blood running through the sculpture itself; it's a large, growing [thing].

**[The setbacks]**
We can start off with the number of setbacks. We couldn't burn in December. Getting tickets here for the artists, having to change those out. Getting the property — being told we couldn't get that property. Airline companies went bankrupt, the ticket agents bellied up, tumultuous weather, major flooding, [we had] to change the dates. These setbacks have actually worked in our favor — the reason being that it's enabled us to actually focus on projects and do projects that take a couple weeks and really get them properly done, instead of having a myriad of projects happening simultaneously and not being able to micromanage each one of them effectively.

**[The participant symbol system]**
Participants of the workshop series are designing all of the symbol systems that are going up — the shape. The reason that we're putting the symbols onto the sculpture here is that these sculptures actually have a resonance with the artists that are working on them. In other words, we aren't telling people what to put on; we're saying put something on that is resonant, something that has meaning to you. We'll be able to read this thing while it's on fire — it'll light up, and everybody's intent will show through.

This is a dragon on the anvil — but the dragon represents also fire, and the blacksmith: without fire he can't… he won't exist. The circle in a square — it means the circle has no beginning and no end, so it means infinity, infinite. If you look at this, there's also lightning coming out of this, representing the energy.

**[The hope]**
The Triple Bypass project will hopefully stimulate the community at large — it'll shine as a symbol of togetherness from one entity, and they'll say "okay, we can get together and make something, and this is what we make — so what else are we going to make together?" I will be happy when I see people coming to the village to help build, to help erect [the work], to create great kitchens together, to feed and work with one another, to really sit around fires and tell stories — to hear an incredible cacophony of sound coming from everywhere, and just a feast of art. That would make me really happy. That's kind of how it happens.
